# Deliverable Routes

Choose the smallest structure that answers the request. These are menus, not mandatory chapter lists.

## Concise industry report

Use when the reader wants a clear mental model and practical judgment.

1. Executive judgment: 5–8 conclusions and their practical meaning.
2. Scope and definitions: only boundaries that prevent misreading.
3. Industry system: users, value chain, channels, money flow, and profit pools.
4. Market state: size/growth only with comparable definitions; lifecycle and structural change.
5. Competition: major participant types, differentiation, barriers, and distribution of outcomes.
6. Product/business logic: value proposition, acquisition, retention, monetization, cost, and cash.
7. Opportunities and risks: conditions, signals, validation, and stop rules.
8. Decision framework: questions, metrics, or a one-page tool the reader can reuse.
9. Gaps and source notes.

Merge sections when the industry is simple. Do not create a chapter solely because the template contains it.

## Company and product brief

Use for a company such as a developer, publisher, platform, or supplier.

1. One-paragraph positioning and current judgment.
2. Proven portfolio: representative products, roles, markets, and lifecycle.
3. Capabilities: what repeated outcomes indicate, with alternative explanations.
4. Strategy and business model: where revenue, distribution, and defensibility come from.
5. Announced/test pipeline: status, evidence date, and uncertainty.
6. Risks and questions to monitor.
7. Lessons transferable to the user's work—and conditions where they are not.

Use a status table for pipelines: `product | genre/market | status | official evidence date | confidence | next signal`. Keep rumors outside the confirmed table.

## Focused research memo

Use for “why,” “whether,” or “how should we decide” questions.

- decision and short answer;
- strongest evidence;
- mechanism and competing explanations;
- scope and counter-evidence;
- action, validation, and monitoring signals;
- unresolved gaps.

## Material-intake note

Use when the user sends a new article, PDF, transcript, or dataset.

- value: high / medium / low;
- evidence type and main limitations;
- affected existing claims or sections;
- route: discard / lead / backend / integrate / section candidate;
- exact revision made or proposed;
- net report-length effect;
- remaining verification need.

## Research backend

Create only assets that serve repeated verification or maintenance. A mature project may use:

- authoritative report;
- source cards;
- claim/evidence ledger;
- terminology and metric dictionary;
- case library with maturity levels;
- research gaps and next-evidence roadmap;
- cognition-change log showing how evidence changed a decision rule.

Do not create all files by default. Begin with the authoritative report plus a compact source/claim ledger, and add backend assets only when repeated use justifies them.

## Final handoff

State:

- the key conclusion or artifact delivered;
- which files or external document were updated;
- what was integrated, replaced, compressed, or rejected;
- important limitations;
- the most valuable next step, if one remains.

## Formal-report permission

Do not make the presentation more certain than the evidence. If a core claim remains conflicted or gapped, still deliver useful supported findings, but title and frame the output as an interim memo or partial assessment. State which missing evidence prevents a full conclusion. Reserve “complete report” language for work whose central questions have crossed the truth and decision gates.
