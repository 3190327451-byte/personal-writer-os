# Evidence and Maintenance Protocol

Use this protocol for multi-source reports, ongoing research projects, contentious claims, or updates to an existing report.

## Research intensity

| Mode | Use when | Minimum retained process |
|---|---|---|
| Quick | One bounded fact, a small comparison, or material triage | Direct answer, best-fit source, limitation if material |
| Standard | Industry/company synthesis or a decision with several moving parts | Claim map, source fit, counter-evidence, gaps, citations |
| Deep | High-cost decision, disputed evidence, unfamiliar market, or formal auditability | Stable claim/source IDs, compact evidence ledger, conflict and gap closure, explicit stop reason |

Increase depth because the decision is consequential or evidence is difficult—not merely because the topic is broad.

## Evidence record

For each consequential claim, retain enough information to audit it:

- claim ID and current wording;
- status: working definition, preliminary, multi-source, disputed, superseded;
- source ID, title, publisher/author, URL or file location;
- event date and publication date;
- geography, channel, product, population, and time boundary;
- sample, method, unit, and definition;
- direct supporting location or short paraphrased span;
- relationship: supports, limits, conflicts, updates, splits, or overturns;
- origin family and relevant commercial interest;
- unresolved gap and evidence that would change the conclusion.

One authoritative primary source can settle a documented fact. A consequential empirical or causal conclusion normally needs independent origins or an explicit single-source limitation. Several articles repeating one dataset count as one origin.

Use these evidence states:

- **supported** — sufficient for the bounded wording used;
- **refuted** — credible evidence supports the opposite or invalidates the claim;
- **conflicted** — comparable evidence still disagrees;
- **gapped** — evidence needed for the intended wording is missing;
- **superseded** — a newer or better-framed claim replaces it.

Only supported or refuted claims should carry a firm conclusion. Conflicted and gapped claims may appear when they matter, but their uncertainty and decision impact must be explicit.

## Source ladder

Use fit-to-claim rather than a universal score:

1. official records, filings, regulator or platform documentation, direct product observation;
2. defined datasets, peer-reviewed research, transparent surveys or experiments;
3. reputable reporting with named evidence and independent verification;
4. industry analysis, conference talks, practitioner interviews;
5. vendor marketing, anonymous claims, community discussion.

Lower tiers can supply hypotheses, language, cases, and leads. They should not silently carry high-impact numeric, causal, legal, financial, or pipeline claims.

## Conflict protocol

When sources disagree:

1. compare definitions, units, periods, regions, samples, and revenue layers;
2. trace both to the original data-generating source;
3. check incentives and whether either source measures a proxy;
4. keep both if they answer different questions;
5. state the unresolved conflict if comparability cannot be restored.

## Pressure test

Before finalizing an important conclusion, ask:

- What is the strongest credible counter-evidence?
- What alternative mechanism could explain the same observation?
- Are we learning from winners while missing the failure distribution?
- Does the conclusion survive a change in region, channel, business model, or period?
- Is a company statement evidence of an action, or only of an intention?
- Which new observation would make us reverse the recommendation?

Pressure testing should revise the conclusion or its boundary, not create an ornamental “limitations” section.

## Admission test for the main report

A finding belongs in the main report only if it does at least one of the following:

- changes a decision, priority, experiment, metric, resource allocation, or stop condition;
- changes the scope, boundary, or confidence of a core conclusion;
- supplies a necessary mechanism, counterexample, or independent evidence;
- resolves a high-priority uncertainty;
- provides a reusable framework that reduces future decision error.

Otherwise keep it in the backend or discard it.

## New-section gate

Create a new main-report section only when all are true:

1. it answers an independent high-value decision question;
2. it cannot be understood cleanly inside an existing section;
3. it has adequate evidence for the strength of its conclusion;
4. it changes an action, framework, or monitoring priority;
5. its learning value exceeds its reading cost.

If any gate fails, update an existing paragraph, note the evidence in the ledger, or leave it as a research gap.

## Compression pass

After every meaningful update:

1. remove superseded claims rather than preserving both as current;
2. merge repeated explanations and definitions;
3. retain the strongest example, not every example;
4. move audit detail, source cards, and calculation notes to the backend;
5. shorten headings to the decision they answer;
6. compare net word or section growth with the new decision value.

The backend may retain history. The authoritative report should show the current best judgment and link to evidence only where useful.

## Stop rule

Stop searching when the important questions are supported to the required confidence, remaining leads are unlikely to change a decision, or the next improvement requires inaccessible data or user-specific evidence. For deep work, record why the search stopped: resolved, diminishing returns, inaccessible evidence, scope/budget boundary, or requires first-party validation. Record the gap and the most efficient next verification step.
